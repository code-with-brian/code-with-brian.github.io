How to read this

Treaty
An agreement between the Crown and Indigenous Nations. The Crown's written text and a Nation's understanding of it can differ. Several Nations on this map say their treaty was never a surrender of land.
Unceded
Land never given up by treaty or other agreement. In this map, the word is used only where the regulator or the Nation uses it.
Consent and consultation
Governments and regulators have a duty to consult Nations whose rights a project may affect. Many Nations hold that their free, prior and informed consent is required. The Chalk River case shows the courts are still working out what the UN Declaration means for consultation.
Who's who
CNSC: the Canadian Nuclear Safety Commission, Canada's nuclear regulator. "The Commission" is its decision-making panel; "CNSC staff" are its regulatory staff. OPG: Ontario Power Generation. CNL: Canadian Nuclear Laboratories, which runs federal nuclear sites for AECL, Atomic Energy of Canada Limited. NWMO: the Nuclear Waste Management Organization. CANDU: Canada's own reactor design. SMR: small modular reactor.

What this map is

A guide to where Canada's nuclear fuel cycle meets treaty and unceded land, built from regulator records, court decisions, company reports and the Nations' own statements. It describes what happened. It is not legal advice, and it doesn't take a position on what is owed.

  • Treaty areas. The shaded areas come from the Government of Canada's Historic Treaties map data, published under the Open Government Licence – Canada. Its boundaries are approximate, it doesn't show unceded territory, and in places it disagrees with how Nations describe their own lands. At Chalk River, for example, the federal map shades Williams Treaties land, while the CNSC and Algonquin Nations call it unceded.
  • Timeline. Each treaty appears in the year it was first signed. Play history steps through twelve moments, with the sources for each listed beside it.
  • Lines. They show the stages in order, not specific shipments. Fuel bundles are made in Port Hope and Peterborough; the CNSC says Peterborough's are for Pickering and Darlington. Pickering, now shut down, has no incoming line.
  • Not pinned. BWXT's Toronto pellet plant and the closed prototype reactors Douglas Point and Gentilly-1 are covered on their neighbours' pages.

Facts are current as of November 23, 2026. If you are from one of these Nations and something here is wrong or missing, please say so and it will be corrected.
