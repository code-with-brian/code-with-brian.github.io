How to read this

Treaty
An agreement between the Crown and Indigenous Nations. The Crown's written text and a Nation's understanding of it can differ. Several Nations on this map say their treaty was never a surrender of land.
Unceded
Land never given up by treaty or other agreement. In this map, the word is used only where the regulator or the Nation uses it.
Consent and consultation
Governments and regulators have a duty to consult Nations whose rights a project may affect. Many Nations hold that their free, prior and informed consent is required. The Chalk River case shows that question is still being decided in court.

What this map is

A guide to where Canada's nuclear fuel cycle meets treaty and unceded land, built from regulator records, court decisions, company reports and the Nations' own statements. It describes what happened. It is not legal advice, and it doesn't take a position on what is owed.

Dots mark sites, not boundaries. Treaty boundaries aren't drawn here, because they are contested and easy to get wrong. The lines show the stages in order, not specific shipments. Fuel bundles are actually made at several Ontario plants, and Pickering, now shut down, has no incoming line. Some sites, like Whiteshell in Manitoba and the fuel plants, aren't included yet.

Facts are current as of November 23, 2026. If you are from one of these Nations and something here is wrong or missing, it will be corrected.
